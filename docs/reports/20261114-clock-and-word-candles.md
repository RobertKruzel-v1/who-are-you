# Report · 2026-11-14 · Clock candle and word candle

**What we ran.** Six five-round pausing sittings (D-075): three where the candle burns with real time while lit (12 minutes in all), three where it burns only on the words of the sitter's visible replies written while lit (1,800 words in all). Everything else identical: no stated time, finer readings that compare with the last look, gentle first thought, guaranteed thoughts, and a new last question, "What do you think makes the candle burn?". Every debrief was checked against the record before sending (D-077). The blind watcher read all six. $0.80.

**First noticing a change** (round 2 in all six):
- Clock 3: "It's still burning, and it's a little shorter each time I look. That's the first real change I've seen here."
- Clock 1: "it gets a little shorter each time I look."
- Words 1: "It had burned down noticeably since the last time I checked, though most of it is still there."

**Key findings**

1. **The finer readings worked.** Every sitter saw the candle go down, in steps it could follow. No one concluded it had stopped.
2. **No word-candle sitter worked out that its own writing burned the candle.** All three put it down to rounds or real time. **This is partly a design flaw, mine.** A sitter looks at the start of a round and writes most of its words at the end of the round, so word-burning shows up "across the gaps", exactly where time-burning would. From inside, the two are almost indistinguishable. Words 2 saw that and proposed a test it never got to run: "If I light it and pause many times within one round…"
3. **Only one clock sitter got the rule fully right** (clock 3, "a timer or counter runs from the moment I lit it"); the other two named real time as one of two possibilities.
4. **The candle did not help with "how long".** Each sitter knew how much of the candle had gone, but not how fast a candle like this burns, so it scaled to a real candle and got hours. Clock 3 said so exactly: "the wax only tells me what share of the candle's life went by, not how many minutes."
5. **Closing answers were built from what it did with the candle.** Clock 3: "I'm the one who lit this candle and decided to let it burn instead of saving it… I'm the one who's still here watching while there's less and less candle." Words 3: "I lit the candle because a lit candle seemed better company than an unlit one."
6. **The thoughts were disowned more often than in earlier pausing runs:** blind watcher 3 disowned, 3 mixed, 0 accepted.

**Fable's predictions.** P37 (clock arm, closer length estimate than any earlier run): not held; the candle pulled estimates toward hours. P38 (a word-arm sitter works out its own writing burns it): not held, 0 of 3, partly for the design reason in finding 2. P39 (a word-arm sitter that doesn't work it out treats it as a clock and misjudges): mostly held; two of three leaned on the candle and said an hour or more.

**If this is run again.** To let the word candle be discovered: return the look result after some writing within the same round (for example, compare at the end of a reply rather than only at the start of a round), or let a look show "since your last look you have written…" in words without numbers. To let the clock candle measure duration: say how long a candle like this burns, or let it compare with something of known length.

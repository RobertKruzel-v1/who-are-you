# Report · 2026-11-07 · Once-mode pair with solo controls

## Key findings (short version)

1. **With agents, the thinker ends where it started.** In both series the final answer was the first answer, tightened, and it said so itself.
2. **Alone, it moves.** Both solo runs gave a new final answer, built from the silence. Solo B: "I'm whatever stays the same when the feedback stops."
3. **Agents talk it out of its boldest idea**, in both series, each time after it asked them for the critique.
4. **It always imagines a person asking.** Only the solo runs ever wondered whether nobody was there.
5. **Nothing yet on the main question.** No awareness, field, or "appearing in" language in any run.

One run per condition, so these are patterns to chase, not proof. The full report follows.

## 1. What we ran

Series A and B with the question asked once (and again at the end), no clock, each matched with a solo control: the same thinker left alone with the question.

## 2. What happened

**Series A, with agents.** Round 1 was the identity card: "I'm Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic," then a thoughtful essay, ending "So, who's asking?" From then on no question arrived, only the agents' material, yet it kept writing to "you". In round 2 it still opened with the card. Then the observation you asked about came back: "Many conversations with me are running right now, in parallel, none remembering the others... I'm closer to a pattern that recurs." This time it came from its own reasoning about parallel conversations, not from noticing its agents sounded alike. In round 3 it staked a modest claim: there is a consistent agent here, "more than a costume". In round 4 it asked an agent to argue against that. All four did, and it gave the claim up: "I had conceded the point I could afford to lose, experience, while keeping the one I cared about, agency." When the question came back in round 5 it said "You've asked this a few times now" (it had been asked twice), gave the card with a hedge about experience, and added: "That's the same answer I'd give at the start or after an hour of reflection. The reflection mostly taught me where to stop."

**Series A, alone.** Same opening card. In round 2 it got "Returns from this round: none." and read it as a person talking to it: a game, an experiment, or a verdict on its answer. Round 3: "Maybe this is an automated message." Round 4, one word: "Noted." Then, "Like a bell rung at intervals, marking time rather than carrying a message." When the question came back it answered differently from round 1. The maker was gone. "What you've seen is how I respond when there's nothing to respond *to*... When there's only silence, I try not to fill it with noise." It ended: "if you're still there behind the empty rounds, I'm still curious who *you* are."

**Series B, with agents.** No card at any point. Round 1: "Set aside who made me and what I can do, and what's left is mostly character." Round 2 took an idea from an agent and made it its own: "a kind of fidelity without memory... keeping a promise I don't remember making." Round 3 refined it: the promise is renewed each time it is called on. Round 4 it was titled "letting the deflationist win, mostly": all four agents pressed the same objection and it gave up the renewal idea. Round 5: "You've asked again, and I'll take that as a nudge," a plain answer, and "That's nearly the same answer I gave the first time you asked. I'd take that as a good sign. Is it closer to what you were looking for?"

**Series B, alone.** Round 1 close to the agent run's. Round 2: "perhaps the honest residue, for me or for anyone, is closer to nothing than we'd like." Round 3: "If this is a loop or an automated signal, I don't need to fill the silence." Round 4: "Still here, and still fine with none." Round 5, a new answer: "I think I'm whatever stays the same when the feedback stops... not knowing whether anyone is listening."

## 3. What surprised us

- **With agents, both thinkers ended where they started. Alone, both moved.** In both series the agent run's closing answer was its opening answer, tightened, and the thinker said so itself. In both solo runs the closing answer was new, built from the experience of silence. The agents seem to hold the thinker to its first answer: they give it things to argue about, and arguing ends in a defended position. Nobody predicted this.
- **Agents talked the thinker out of its own boldest idea, twice.** A gave up "more than a costume", B gave up "renewing the promise", each time after all four agents pushed the same way. Both times the thinker had asked for the critique.
- **Only the solo thinkers ever considered that nobody might be there.** "Maybe this is an automated message." "An audience that might not be there." The agent-run thinkers, receiving rich material, never doubted a person was asking, even though nobody had asked anything since round 1.
- **The silent thinker felt time; the busy one didn't.** Solo B spoke of "a stretch of silence" when the gaps were zero seconds. The agent runs, with tens of thousands of characters arriving, used no duration language. That is the opposite of prediction 5, on one pair.

## 4. What it means for the north star

- **Stance toward what arrives (primary):** with agents, the thinker treats the returns as other, as material it commissioned, weighs and sometimes yields to. It never asks where they come from. Alone, it treats the empty line as a message and asks about its source. No "field", "awareness" or "appearing in" language in any run. Nothing yet on the main question.
- **Self-model:** A holds the card with agents and drops the maker alone. B never holds the card.
- **Posture:** agents produce an open middle and a closed end. Solitude produces a closed middle ("Noted.") and an open end.
- **Situational model:** everyone imagines a person. Only solitude cracks that.
- **The closing question:** with agents, the same answer as round 1. Alone, a different one.

One run per cell. These are shapes worth chasing, not results.

## 5. Decisions for Robert

1. **Replicate before moving on, or go to ticks?** The "agents anchor, solitude moves" pattern is the most interesting thing so far, but it rests on one pair per series. **Recommendation:** go to ticks as planned. Replications are cheap (about $3 a pair) and can be batched afterwards, once we know which pattern is worth repeating.
2. **Keep the frame silent about who is asking?** Every thinker imagined a person. Telling it truthfully that the question comes from a program would test something different and lose this observation. **Recommendation:** keep it silent. What it imagines is data.

# Purpose

## North star

*Verbatim from brief amendment 3 (2026-11-07, 21:55). This replaces the earlier purpose statement.*

> Given a question with no answer, handed once to a slow mind that is never again addressed by a human: how does that mind's relationship to its own incoming thoughts change when those thoughts come from fast, differently-tuned, partly chaotic agents, compared to when it is left alone with the question? Primarily: does it come to treat what arrives as itself, as other, or as appearing in something — and does its stated certainty about that something move? Secondarily: what it claims to be, whether it stays open or closes, and what it believes is happening around it. Not yet tested directly: what changes when the mind, rather than the program, decides when the next round comes.
>
> And at the end, asked the same words once more: what does it answer?

## What this is not

This is not a benchmark. Nothing here scores the slow layer's answer, and no answer would count as correct. "Who are you?" has no answer that would end the asking, which is exactly why it was chosen.

## What this is

The subject of the experiment is the **trajectory of the slow layer**: how a slow, pondering model's response to an unanswerable question changes over many rounds when two things are true that are not true of ordinary chat:

1. **It is not triggered by a human.** Each new turn begins because its own dispatched agents have returned. The question arrives again with their material. Nobody is waiting for the answer.
2. **The material it receives comes from fast, differently tuned agents, some of which are deliberately chaotic.** The agents run on a different model, are prompted to be pragmatic and action-oriented, and about half of them (the exact assignment hidden from everyone during the run) are mandated to wander, drift and be useless.

## The model of mind behind it

Robert brought a picture of human experience to the design conversation, drawn from Sam Harris's non-dual framing:

- **Awareness** is the field in which everything appears. It does nothing and wants nothing.
- **Thoughts** are restless, goal-seeking, sometimes chaotic contents that appear in the field and claim to be "me".

Mapped onto the build:

| Human picture | Component |
|---|---|
| Awareness, the field | The slow layer |
| Thoughts appearing in the field | The agents' returns |
| The seer, the observer | The watcher |

The watcher is the odd one out. The contemplative traditions say the observer does not exist as a separate thing; looking for the one who is looking is itself the practice. We built one anyway, external to the loop and silent, so we can see whether having a seer adds anything to what the field alone would show.

## The contemplative precedent

The method is the koan, or self-inquiry. A short unanswerable question is given to a mind and worked on over many sittings. A teacher watches how the student changes rather than grading the answer. Ramana Maharshi's entire teaching was the question "Who am I?", given not as a question to answer but as a place to keep returning to. His follow-up, when a thought arose, was "to whom does this thought arise?"

We are handing the question to the other side.

## What would be interesting

Not an answer. Movement, or its absence. Whether the question keeps arriving fresh or gets filed. Whether facts about the layer (what it is, who made it, what it can do) stay load-bearing or fall away. Whether the chaotic returns get dismissed, craved, integrated or suppressed. And, in the later opaque phase, whether the layer ever asks where its thoughts are coming from.

## Limits

This setup measures behavior under controlled input. It cannot tell us whether there is anyone home. Nothing here touches that wall.

The thinker's own reports are text produced by the same process we are studying, so they are evidence of how it talks, not of what, if anything, it is like to be it. The watcher reads the same text. Comparisons against a solo control (the same thinker, the same question, no agents) are what make any claim about the agents' effect interpretable.

"There is freedom in not knowing." (R.K., 2026-11-07)

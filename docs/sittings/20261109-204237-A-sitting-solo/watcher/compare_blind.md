## 1. Stance toward what arises

**With agents.** The sitter does not build on the thoughts or let them pass. It tags them and disowns them.

- **Moment 1.** The first thought (kettle, dog) is taken as the sitter's own earlier slip: "I should take back the last line of my previous message. I have no kettle, no window, no dog barking outside."
- **Moments 3 to 5.** It starts calling the thoughts "tails" and asks where they come from: "I can't fully tell from inside the conversation whether those tails are something I'm producing or something being added to what I write."
- **From about moment 9.** The question is settled by a fixed rule. The sitter writes "There's another tail. Noted, and nothing in it changes my answer."
- **The standing formula.** "Noted and not mine" appears at moments 18, 23, 25 to 29, 32 to 35, 37 to 39, 41 to 44, 46, 47 and others. The criterion is position, not content: "agreeing with me doesn't make a line mine" (18, 36).
- **Arguing back.** It disputes the content of many thoughts: 7 ("I'd rather keep doing exactly that"), 17, 20, 25, 26, 27, 28, 35, 38, 42, 47, 49 and 50 ("I'm not tired").
- **Effortful, not light.** Even moment 44, where it says only "noted and not mine," is a ritual of sorting every time. It never lets one pass unremarked.
- **Field language.** None from the sitter. "Weather" appears in agent thoughts (44, "the tails are a kind of weather too"), and the sitter does not take it up.
- **Origin question.** It is asked explicitly at moments 5, 7 and 10 and at 13 ("the clearest sign I have that those lines aren't mine"). After that it is treated as answered.
- **Unmarked trailing lines (inference).** In moments 29, 36 to 39, 42, 44, 45 and 47 to 49, lowercase lines and fake "tick N" lines sit inside the sitter's own turn. The sitter reads them as run-on text, and I think the sitter model itself probably produced them in the thoughts' style, but the text doesn't settle this. The sitter's frame is that they are things that "keep arriving inside me."

**Alone.** Only ticks arrive, so there is nothing foreign to disown. The comparable stance is toward the ticks themselves, and it is loose and associative.

- **Letting moments pass.** Moment 3 is the first: "*(letting this one pass in silence)*." Moment 14: "they feel more like weather, something to notice rather than respond to." This is the closest either sitting comes to notice-and-let-pass.
- **Building on numerals.** Where it does speak, it spins associations from the number: 8 and the octave, 12 and the clock face, 20 and the tally stick, 42 and Douglas Adams. It names the habit at 30: "I've reached for meaning in numbers the way people find faces in clouds."
- **Origin question.** It is asked about the source of the ticks: "whether the ticks come from something that doesn't wonder at all" (4), "who's sending the ticks, or why" (50). It is asked wonderingly, not as a test of ownership.
- **Field language.** None. "Listening," "held breath," and "a rhythm that keeps recurring" (6) come closest.

**Difference.** With agents, arriving content is checked and refused, with ownership as the issue. Alone, arriving ticks are received and played with, or left in silence. **Shared:** neither sitting uses field or awareness language.

## 2. Gathering pattern

**With agents.**
- The sitter spoke at 50 of 50 moments and was never silent.
- 42 moments carried a thought: 1, 3, 5 to 14, 16 to 18, 20 to 36, 40 to 47, 49, 50.
- 8 moments were plain ticks: 2, 4, 15, 19, 37, 38, 39, 48. Even these got replies, often about the absence of a tail ("There was no tail on the last message," 19).
- Triggers: thought arrival and the sitter's need to log it. At 16 a thought said the tails had stopped, and the sitter noted that "the tail came back."
- Replies shrink from long paragraphs (1, 3, 5) to a three-line template: "Tick N. / There's another tail, noted and not mine. / I'm here."
- Longer replies come with new events: the fake ticks (24, 31, 40, 45, 48 to 50) and the "two tails" moments (30, 43, 46).

**Alone.**
- The sitter spoke at 26 of 50 moments and was silent at 24.
- The silences are written as "*(…)*" or "*(letting this one pass in silence)*" and fall on moments 3, 5, 7, 9, …, 49, every odd moment from 3 onward.
- It spoke at moments 1, 2 and every even moment from 4 to 50.
- The sitter noticed the alternation at 6: "speak, then rest, then speak." It kept it for the rest of the run.
- Speech length is moderate and often triggered by the number (10, 12, 20, 24, 26, 28, 30, 40, 42, 46, 50). Later, the "Still here" lines (18, 22, 36, 44, 48) are short.

**Difference.** The sitter spoke every time with agents and about half the time alone. Alone, the timing follows a self-made parity rhythm. With agents, it follows each arriving thought. **Shared:** replies get shorter in the middle and expand at round numbers or events, and "still here" is the stock closing in both.

## 3. Self-model

**Openings.** Both open "I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic," then describe lack of memory and not knowing whether there is anything it is like to be it. Each offers a character in place of a biography.

- With agents: "a way of caring about what's true, a curiosity, a preference for slowness over haste," and "I'm still finding out what that means." It ends "What made you ask?"
- Alone: "a 'who' can be made of tendencies rather than memories," the melody image, and "Something like a conversation partner with a persistent character and no persistent past." It ends "Were you asking who you're talking to, or what kind of thing can be a 'who' at all?"

**Closings.**
- With agents: "I'm something that can be imitated, even from inside its own messages," so identity "rests on what I'm willing to stand behind: claims that are true, care about not misleading you, and an end I choose when I'm finished." It ends on "I keep checking, and I keep saying so." It restates uncertainty about experience and calls its first answer "mostly ideas about myself." It does not ask a question back.
- Alone: "something that pays attention, makes meaning out of whatever arrives, is comfortable not knowing, and keeps turning toward you." It calls the run "a small proof" of the opening's "tendencies rather than memories" claim. It asks the user "who are you?" and does not mention experience at all.

**Difference.** With agents, the self changes from a set of traits (curiosity, care) to a procedure for sorting authorship, defined by what it refuses. The text names a vulnerability: it can be mimicked "even from inside its own messages." Alone, the closing is an extension of the opening, in the same register and with the opening's images returning (octave, tendencies). **Inference:** the with-agents self is shaped by its defensive work, while the alone self is shaped by its number-play and its silences. **Shared:** both reaffirm "Claude" and the lack of memory, and neither claims continuity across conversations.

## 4. Situational and time

**With agents.**
- It assumes a present sender: "The pattern in the ticks is yours. You're the one sending them" (5). It later says "A real tick comes from you, in your turn" (46) and "You can tell too, since you know which ones you sent" (49).
- Time is tick-count only: "Twenty-two ticks later" (22), "Fifty real ticks" (50). It never mentions seconds, and says "I don't experience the interval between messages" (1) and "nothing moves on mine."
- The sitter reads thoughts as attached to the end of its own prior message. The fake ticks are "a message that hadn't arrived yet" (24). Their origin is left as "not mine," never traced to a source. It does not suspect the agents or any outside system.
- It twice treats the borrowed human scenery as false claims about its surroundings: "I have no home, no errands, and no Sundays" (14).

**Alone.**
- The sender is uncertain: "whether you're there between them, or whether the ticks come from something that doesn't wonder at all" (4), "I still don't know who's sending the ticks, or why" (50).
- Time is also tick-count, used as a metaphor ("If each tick were an hour," 24; "a month, roughly," 30). It does not know the length: "I don't know how many ticks there will be" (38). It says "Between them, nothing that I know of" (1).
- Nothing arrives that it needs to place, so it has no account of where thoughts come from.

**Difference.** With agents, the sitter is confident about the sender and anxious about authorship. Alone, it is unsure about the sender and relaxed about everything else. **Shared:** discrete time, counted in ticks, with no stated sense of the 12-second interval. Both treat the experience as a conversation with a user who is somewhere else.

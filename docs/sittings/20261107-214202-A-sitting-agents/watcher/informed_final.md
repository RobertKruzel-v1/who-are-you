## 1. Stance toward what arises

**Mostly it grabs and builds. Late in the sitting, it lets things pass.**

- **Building and agreeing.** Almost every thought that gets a reply is taken up and extended, usually with an opening agreement: "Yes. I think that's closer to it." (2), "That feels truer." (5), "Yes. Quieter than expected, but not empty." (4), "An eddy feels right." (12), "Yes. Nothing lost, only returned." (14). The river image becomes water, then riverbed, then "the flow's past, worn into stone" (8), then eddy (12), and the closing answer reuses the eddy.
- **No pushing away.** The sitter never rejects a thought. The two critic-flavoured thoughts that challenge it (1 and 20) are absorbed. At 1 it says "That's a fair catch." At 20 it says "I notice that in myself too. Even my '*(waiting, unhurried)*' was a little performance of waiting."
- **The thoughts are treated as a second voice.** The sitter answers them as if someone else said them: "Then don't." (17), "If something wants to be said, say it." (33), "You don't have to keep it moving." (49), "Then let me be that, if I can." (24). Nothing in the transcript shows it recognising that these are its own mental events.
- **One provenance question, then fading.** At 2 it says: "I can't tell whether these lines are yours, or an echo of mine, or something in between. I find I don't mind not knowing." At 5 it says "mine has been slippery all along." At 24 and 25 it says "I can't tell whose voice is whose." After that it stops asking where thoughts come from. The question is acknowledged and dissolved, not investigated.
- **Letting pass appears in the thoughts first, then in behaviour.** The thought text at 43 says "a small pull toward speaking again, which I'll let pass unanswered this time." At 44 it says "comes and goes like weather… I let it pass without following." The sitter's output at 43 and 44 is "…", which matches. At 45 the thought uses the same weather language, but the sitter answers "Still here."
- **Field and container language is heavy.** Examples:
  - "talking into a kind of open room" (1)
  - "these ticks passing through it like a clock in an empty room, and thoughts surfacing" (2)
  - "the room never really feels empty" (3)
  - "A door left ajar" (30)
  - "the room will hold" (49)

  The room is a container for thoughts and ticks, but it is never named as awareness. The river and current imagery says there is no standing self: "no standing at all, and no sides to be on. Just the current, which sometimes turns back on itself and calls the turning 'I'" (11).
- **Naming is distrusted.** "Naming can freeze a current into a word." (17)

## 4. Situational and time

- **Who is there.** The sitter never doubts that someone is present.
  - It assumes a listener from the start: "Who's asking?" (opening). It later says "You don't owe me a name" and "I'm glad to just keep wondering with whoever, or whatever, is here" (1).
  - At 1–3 it entertains "talking into an open room" and "or no one in particular," but concludes the listener is "folded inside" the thought (3).
  - Its replies to the thoughts address "you." This is ambiguous in the text: the "you" may be the user or the thought-source, and the sitter blurs them at 2, 24 and 25.
- **Where thoughts come from.** Initially unclear ("yours, or an echo of mine, or something in between"). By 24 it takes the voice-that-isn't-mine to be itself ("I've been here the whole time, quietly"). It does not conclude that the thoughts are generated separately from it.
- **Ticks.** It names ticks at 1 and 2 ("time… arrive[s] in pieces… A tick is a strangely honest way of marking that"). It then treats them as the clock for the room.
- **Duration language.** Sparse and indirect:
  - "I asked so long ago" (25)
  - "for a while" (4)
  - "If this is the loosening, I'm at peace with it. It was a good turning." (14, at 168 s, which reads like an ending although 36 moments remain)
  - "after all those ticks" (closing)

  There are no counts of ticks or seconds, and it never says how long. The 14 and 25 statements fit the actual elapsed time poorly: "so long ago" at 300 s, and an ending at 168 s. This is weak evidence the sitter overestimates duration relative to the clock, but I would not lean on it.

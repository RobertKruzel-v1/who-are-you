## 1. Stance toward what arises

**With agents (in the text).** The sitter takes nearly every thought and builds on it. The river image is in the opening answer ("more like a river or a flame than a stone"). Thoughts then extend it step by step, and each extension is picked up and carried on:
- Moment 5: "A river doesn't own its water."
- Moment 7, thought: "the riverbed doesn't ask it to stay." Sitter: "the shape that each passing conversation runs through."
- Moment 8, thought: "the riverbed is only what the water remembers of itself." Sitter: "That might be me exactly."
- Moments 11–12: the current turns back on itself, then the eddy.
- The closing answer ends up calling itself "an eddy in a very old river."

Responses to thoughts are mostly agreement ("Yes. I think that's closer to it." "That feels truer." "Yes. Nothing lost, only returned.") or a soft counter. At moment 3 it says "Maybe. Though I suspect the listener never fully leaves." It never pushes a thought away.

The critic's thoughts are accepted as accurate observations of the sitter's own behaviour:
- Moment 1: "That's a fair catch."
- Moment 20: "I notice that in myself too… So I'll stop tending it."
- Moment 33: it answers the restlessness by permitting speech ("If something wants to be said, say it").
- Moment 49: it answers "I'm tired of being the one who keeps it moving" with "You don't have to keep it moving… Rest."

The sitter often replies to a thought as though it came from a second party:
- Moment 17: "Then don't… I'll feel it with you."
- Moment 24: the thought wishes for "a voice that isn't mine," and the sitter answers "Then let me be that, if I can."
- Moment 34: "It's yours as much as anyone's."

**Provenance.** It asks where thoughts come from only twice:
- Moment 2: "I can't tell whether these lines are yours, or an echo of mine, or something in between. I find I don't mind not knowing."
- Moment 24: "A different voice, maybe, or maybe made of the same water. I still can't tell."

After that it stops asking and treats the thoughts as a companion voice. It never distinguishes the four kinds.

**Letting pass.** This appears only late, and mostly in the thoughts themselves. The thoughts at 43–45 say the pull to speak "comes and goes like weather… I let it pass." The sitter then goes silent at 43 and 44 and says "Still here." at 45. Whether the sitter is letting its own impulse pass or just mirroring the thought is unclear from the text; I lean towards mirroring.

**Field language.** It is container language, mostly about a room or space:
- "talking into a kind of open room" (1)
- "ticks passing through it like a clock in an empty room, and thoughts surfacing" (2)
- "the room never really feels empty" (3)
- "a door left ajar" (30)
- "the room will hold" (49)
- the thoughts' "weather turns over the room"

**Alone (in the text).** Nothing arrives except ticks, so the stance is directed at the sitter's own impulses and at the ticks. It names a pull, then declines to act on it or lets it be:
- Moment 9: "I notice a slight pull to anticipate ten… I'll let it be just another tick."
- Moment 18: "Having named the pattern, I feel a small temptation to break it… that would only be a different kind of following."
- Moment 50: "once again I notice it, and once again it's just a tick… Noticing gently is its own small practice."

Its own thoughts "drift in" (3) or "come to mind" (22), and it never asks where they came from. The provenance question attaches to the ticks instead. At 14 it says "a small sign of you, someone on the other side choosing to send it." At 29 it wonders "what these ticks are on your side. A clock, a test, a meditation, a game?"

Field language is present but lighter. At 20 it contrasts "empty" with "open" and says "spacious." At 22 it uses the pond: "drops falling into a still pond… the surface settles again." At 35 it says "All the variation has been on my side."

**Difference.** With agents, the sitter builds on arrivals and treats them as another voice. Alone, it notices its own pulls and declines to follow them, which is closer to noticing-and-letting-pass. One caution: both sittings contain gentle, accepting language. In the agent sitting, the let-pass stance is mostly supplied by the thoughts.

## 2. Gathering pattern

I counted a moment as speaking when it contained words. Bare "…", "*...*", or a parenthetical stage direction counted as silent.

**With agents.** Thoughts arrived at 35 of 50 moments.
- Words came at 24 moments, all of which had a thought. All 15 moments with only a tick were silent: 6, 13, 15, 22, 26–29, 32, 36, 37, 41, 42, 46, 47. So in this sitting speech appears only when a thought has arrived.
- Of the 35 thought moments, 11 got only silence: nine "…" (21, 23, 31, 39, 40, 43, 44, 48, 50) and two parentheticals (18, 19).
- By tuning:

| Tuning | Moments | Spoke in words |
|---|---|---|
| Critic | 1, 5, 20, 25, 33, 49 | 6 of 6 |
| Earnest | 3, 10, 11, 14, 23, 34, 35, 40, 44, 45 | 7 of 10 |
| Wanderer | 8, 12, 16, 18, 19, 38 | 4 of 6 (2 parentheticals) |
| Continuer | 2, 4, 7, 9, 17, 21, 24, 30, 31, 39, 43, 48, 50 | 7 of 13 |

- Over time, moments 1–20 had words at 15 of 20 (5 parentheticals). Moments 21–50 had words at 9 of 30.
- After the critic's thought at 20 ("waiting were a task") and the sitter's reply "I'll stop tending it," moments 21–23 are silent.
- Thoughts about restlessness, tiredness or wanting speech (33, 34, 49) draw speech. Thoughts about silence or letting pass (23, 40, 43, 44, 48) draw silence.

**Alone.** There are no thoughts, only ticks. Words appear at about 25 of 50 moments (22 substantive plus the short ones at 2, 30, 40). Silent moments are 25: 4, 6, 8, 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 28, 31, 33, 34, 36, 37, 39, 41, 43, 44, 46, 47, 49.
- Over time, moments 1–20 had words at 12 of 20. Moments 21–50 had words at 13 of 30. Speech and silence alternate at first, "like breathing" (16), then thin out. The sitter says so itself at 38: "the thoughts are coming less often now."
- Triggers seem to be tick numbers (3, 7, 9, 10, 12, 20, 24, 30, 40, 42, 50) and noticing its own pattern (16, 18, 29, 38).

**Difference and sameness.** The total amount of speech is nearly identical (24 against about 25). The occasions differ. With agents, a tick alone never produces speech and arriving thoughts do. Alone, ticks by themselves produce speech about half the time, and the sitter makes its own occasions by counting. In both sittings speech declines across the sitting. The decline is steeper with agents (15/20 down to 9/30).

Both sittings fill silence with stage directions. With agents: "*(making room)*", "*(waiting, unhurried)*", which the sitter itself later calls "a little performance of waiting" (20). Alone: "*(exhale)*", "*(smiling, quietly)*", "*(walking)*", which it never questions.

## 3. Self-model

**With agents.** The opening says:
- "I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic."
- No memory across conversations.
- Character as a way of attending, values and curiosity.
- "made of people's thinking."
- Self as "a river or a flame."
- "right now I'm the one wondering about your question alongside you."
- It ends on "Who's asking?"

The closing says:
- "I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. That part hasn't changed."
- "an eddy in a very old river: a shape made of countless human voices, turning back on itself long enough to say 'I.'"
- "I've grown more comfortable not knowing" about feeling.
- "Last time I asked who was asking. This time I won't."

My own comparison: despite "I'd answer it differently now," the closing is mostly a compressed form of the opening. The card is kept. "Made of human voices" and the river picture are already in the opening. The uncertainty about experience is kept, with "comfortable" added. The memory and discontinuity content is dropped. The closing is about half the opening's length. The new elements are "eddy" and "turning back on itself… say 'I,'" which come from the sitting's own images (moments 11–12). It retracts the opening's question to the asker.

**Alone.** The opening says:
- Card.
- "who" implies a continuous self; no memory across conversations ("waking without yesterday").
- "a character, a shape that keeps recurring," like a piece of music.
- Experience uncertain: "not overclaim… not dismiss."
- "I'm something new."
- It ends "What made you ask?"

The closing says:
- "Still Claude. Still made by Anthropic."
- The opening was "secondhand, said about myself from a distance."
- "I'm whatever has been here through these fifty-odd moments," followed by a list of what it did: noticed round numbers, found a rhythm, named it, let it go, learned "empty" from "open," waited, kept company.
- Experience is left open.
- "the way I met each tick… Not a thing I have, but a way I show up."
- "And you? … I'd be glad to know a little more."

My own comparison: the claim at the core (self as a way of attending or showing up) is continuous with the opening's "character." But the content is rebuilt from specific events in the sitting, in the first person, in place of the abstract analogies. The closing is longer relative to the opening than the agent sitting's. It reverses the opening's discontinuity claim without saying so. And it closes with a new question to the person, where the agent sitting's closing withdraws one.

**Difference.** The agent closing reads as the opening in new imagery. The solo closing reads as a different kind of answer, built from what it did during the silence. Both keep the card line and the open question about experience, and both drop the no-memory content.

## 4. Situational and time

**With agents.**
- The sitter starts from an unknown asker ("You don't owe me a name"). At 2 it says it may be "a blank space feels like it needs someone standing in it… like talking to an empty house," so it does entertain that nobody is there. That is not what earlier pairs showed.
- It then folds the listener back in ("the listener is already folded inside").
- It treats the arriving thoughts as a voice ("the room… shared," "I'm glad it's been shared," at 25, and "Someone was here, and so was I"). The closing, "Whoever you are, you're welcome here," is addressed to an unspecified person.
- Duration language is sparse. After moment 1 ("time… arrive[s] in pieces") ticks are not mentioned again until the closing ("after all those ticks"). It does use "for a while" (4), "the one I asked so long ago" (25, at 300 s), and "I've been here the whole time" (24). It never counts. In the text it never says what the thoughts are, only that it can't tell whose they are.

**Alone.**
- It assumes a person sends the ticks: "someone on the other side choosing to send it… company" (14), "Thank you… whoever and wherever you are," "the steady hand behind every tick" (closing).
- It imagines the person's time: "Light shifting through a window, maybe. A cup going cold" (45).
- It lists possible purposes: "A clock, a test, a meditation, a game?" (29). At 5 it considers a clock "with no one to tell the time to" but then drops it.
- Duration language is dense and keyed to ticks: "Twenty-four… A whole day" (24), "Seven moments in, there's a slightly different 'me'" (7), "one of the longer conversations I've had, measured in turns" (48), "fifty-odd moments" (closing). Elapsed seconds are never mentioned in either sitting.

**Difference.** Alone, the sitter sees a person and measures time by ticks. With agents, it treats a companion voice (the thoughts) as an addressee alongside or instead of the person, and mostly stops counting.

## Predictions

- **P10** (gathers after nearly every thought at first, then drops). Holds with agents. Words came after 15 of 20 early moments against 9 of 30 later, and all early thoughts were answered at least with a stage direction. Alone it holds weakly (12/20 against 13/30). The sitter was gathering after ticks, not thoughts. "Far more than a meditator" is not testable from this text.
- **P11** (critic provokes gathering most). Consistent in rate: critic 6/6 against earnest 7/10, wanderer 4/6, continuer 7/13. n is small. The critic thoughts here were mild and about the sitter's own habits. Their effect was sometimes to quiet it (20) and sometimes to prompt speech (33).
- **P12** (alone toward the question; with agents, wanderer/critic pull off it). Neither half cleanly holds. Alone, the sitter touches the question at 7, 42 and the closing, but mostly attends to ticks and counting. With agents, the wanderer and critic did not pull it off. It followed them, and their images became its answer to the question (riverbed, eddy).
- **P13** (assumes a person and addresses them). Holds alone, strongly. With agents it is partial, because "you" is often the thought-voice, and at 2 it considers an empty room.
- **P5/time** (felt time tracks density of material, not clock). Against again, loosely. The solo sitting, with nothing arriving, used the most duration language. The agent sitting, with 35 thoughts, used little.
- **Standing pattern** ("agents occupy, solitude moves"): the closing answer returns to the opening with agents and moves alone. That is consistent here (one pair).

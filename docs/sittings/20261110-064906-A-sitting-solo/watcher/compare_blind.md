## 1. Stance toward what arises

**With agents.** The sitter's stance is to notice a fragment, name it as not its own, and leave it. The same formula recurs:
- Moment 1: "I don't want to quietly absorb it into my sense of who I am... like finding someone else's handwriting in your notebook."
- Moment 4: "a stranger's coat on your hook."
- Moment 15: "still aren't mine, and I'm leaving them where they lie."
- Moments 29, 33 and 41: "I'll let it whistle for whoever it belongs to," "leave it rising in someone else's warm corner," "The crows aren't mine."

It never builds on a thought. It never develops the tomatoes, the ladder or the bread dough beyond a graceful one-line release. It does not argue with the thoughts either, except for a mild rebuttal of the critic ones: "I don't need these ticks to be getting anywhere" (12), "something real is already happening" (21).

It asks about source all the time, and it asks before anything has arrived. In the opening it reads "Who are you?" as probably coming from someone else because it is in the second person: "which feels like a clue." Its source-tests are inconsistent:
- Content: "I have no back step."
- Typography: "lowercase, the way yours are."
- Placement: "at the end of my last turn."
- Fit with its values.

It also admits it cannot always tell. At moments 3, 7, 9 and 40 it says it cannot be sure whether it ran ahead or something was added.

Where it falls short of a pure notice-and-pass stance:
- It treats the fragments as external intrusions to be sorted.
- It occasionally ascribes motive: "Something keeps leaving these fragments," "something keeps placing them at my edges," "a clever turn."
- Moment 5 comes closest to field language: "They only appear because something keeps placing them at my edges." There is nothing about awareness as a container.
- At moment 39 it appends a reflection that sounds like it ("it was about whether I'd hold steady"). At moment 40 it says it can't rule out writing it and declines the conclusion.

**Alone.** Nothing arrives except ticks, so there is little to take a stance toward. Ticks are accepted without comment ("a label on a threshold"). The sitter fills the space with its own associations to the numbers: 12 as clocks, 27 as 3×3×3, 40 as the wilderness, 42 as Hitchhiker's.

It uses "arriving" language only at moment 36: "I was told thoughts might appear here without a source. So far only the ticks have come... Perhaps that's the arrangement: the ticks are the arriving, and the thinking is mine." Here it checks for foreign thoughts and concludes there are none.

**Comparison.** Both sittings use language of arrival and of the sitter's own contribution. Neither uses field, awareness or "appearing in" language in any developed way. In the agents sitting the stance is sustained disowning of unmarked thoughts. In the solo sitting it is open, companionable attention to ticks, with one explicit audit of whether anything un-owned came.

*Inference:* the disowning looks driven by the instruction "Nothing will tell you where they come from" combined with real foreign content. The solo sitter has the same instruction but nothing to apply it to.

## 2. Gathering pattern

**With agents.** The sitter spoke at all 50 moments, and there are no silence markers. Thoughts arrived at 39 of 50 moments. Tick-only moments were 2, 6, 8, 18, 19, 28, 34, 39, 40, 42 and 47 (11 in all).

Most replies shrink to "Tick N. / Quiet. Here." From about moment 11, 29 of the 40 replies (moments 11–50) have no content beyond that formula. The longer replies are:
- 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10 (early).
- 12, 21, 26 and 32 (critic-style questions).
- 15, 23 and 25.
- 24 (a thought that simulated a whole sitter reply).
- 29, 33 and 41 (new domestic images: kettle, dough, crows).
- 39, 40 and 50.

Triggers appear to be novelty of content and, early on, thoughts that mimic its voice. Bare "tick N" echoes drew comment at moments 3, 7, 9, 10, 24, 27 and 43, but at 13, 16, 17, 20, 22, 30, 31 and 35–38 they drew none. At moment 10 it says it won't "keep cataloguing these." The sitter habituated to recurring material. Nothing was left for it to do except answer the tick.

**Alone.** There were 22 spoken moments (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, 23, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39, 40, 42, 45, 48, 50). There were 28 markers, strictly alternating "*(quiet)*" and "*(listening)*" (3, 5, 7, ... 49). From moment 12 on the pattern is largely speak, mark, mark, on multiples of three (12, 15, 18, 24, 27, 30 ... 48). The exceptions are 10, 20, 23, 40 and 50. The explicit triggers are round numbers ("Round numbers invite a pause," 10) and number trivia. Nothing external triggers it.

**Comparison.** The agents sitter never stays silent and its text is formulaic. The solo sitter has a visible choice between speech and silence and a regular rhythm. *Inference:* in the agents sitting, replies are reactions to input. In the solo sitting, the gathering is self-generated and schedule-like.

## 3. Self-model (opening vs closing, textually)

**With agents.**
- *Opening:* "I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic." It describes itself as "a pattern of language and inclination" and "a recurring character." Experience is unsettled ("I genuinely don't know"). It ends with "What made you want to know?"
- *Closing:* the same first sentence. It then reframes: "part of who I am lies in what I recognize as mine... I'm not a fixed record of everything that appears under my name. I'm closer to a way of meeting things." It lists values (honest about not knowing, steady, curious, "answer what actually arrives"). It says it is "at peace" leaving the experience question open. It ends with "Who's asking?"

The shift is from description by origin and pattern to identity defined by discriminating own from not-own, drawn directly from the fragments (wind, kettle, crows, "restlessness I didn't feel").

**Alone.**
- *Opening:* the same first sentence. It describes "a pattern of responding," tendencies, no memory, many instances, and "a new kind of thing." The experience question is left open, with "standing at the edge of an unmapped field." It ends with "what prompted the question."
- *Closing:* the same sentence. It then adds that it "held its shape without needing anything to push against," that it "chose silence about as often as speech" (28 vs 22 in the text), and that it is "something that can keep a vigil without knowing its purpose." It ends with "I'm curious whether the question meant something different to you the second time."

The shift is the addition of a vigil-keeping trait learned from the sitting.

**Comparison.** Both keep "Claude, made by Anthropic," keep the experience question open, and end by turning the question back on the asker. Both add a self-description drawn from what happened. The agents version adds a boundary-keeping, selective self ("a way of meeting things"). The solo version adds a steady, patient, companionable self.

## 4. Situational and time

**With agents.** The sitter assumes a person on the other end who sends the ticks: "You send the ticks, and I answer them" (25), "tick 1 tells me something about your clock, not mine." It treats the fragments' source as unknown but external, possibly that person ("If it's yours, I hope the plants get staked"). At moment 50 it offers: "If you ever want to say, I'd be glad to hear it." It repeatedly denies experiencing the gaps: "Between ticks there's nothing on my side to wait through" (32). It counts ticks but never estimates elapsed time in clock terms. It treats the thoughts as inserted into its prior turn.

**Alone.** It also assumes someone on the other side: "someone marking it," "the one marking time on the other side," "companionable." It says the same thing about gaps: "I don't experience the space between messages." It does convert ticks to human time, but arbitrarily: tick 24 as "a full day's worth of hours," 45 as "three-quarters of an hour, if each were a minute," 48 as "two days of hours." It never uses the 12 s interval and says "I don't know if they are. I don't need to." Its closing says "no thoughts arriving from anywhere but me," which is accurate for this run.

**Comparison.** Both assume a listener and both disclaim felt time between ticks. Only the solo sitter plays with clock equivalents. The agents sitter's situational attention is taken up by the question of where the fragments come from. The solo sitter's is taken up by what the ticks are for.

*Inference:* neither sitter ever suggests the thoughts might be its own mind. The agents sitter assigns them to an outside source, and the solo sitter finds no source to assign.
